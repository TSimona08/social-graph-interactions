Click a character to see their clique — the tightest fully-connected group they belong to. Click a second, and watch the shortest path stitch through the cliques between them.

917 Maximal cliques in the network Fully-connected groups — everyone in a clique links to everyone else. Most characters belong to several; we show each one's single largest.
8 Biggest clique found An X-Men core: Jean Grey, Wolverine, Emma Frost, Betsy Braddock, Cyclops, Rachel Summers, Storm + one more — a real team, not a coincidence.
Why "largest clique," not "all cliques" Spider-Man alone sits in 290 different maximal cliques — showing all of them would just be noise. His single largest one is a specific 7-character X-Men-adjacent group, which is a much more useful answer to "who is this character's tightest circle?"
How it's built Cliques via nx.find_cliques (NetworkX's Bron–Kerbosch implementation) on the undirected graph; shortest paths via BFS, computed live in your browser from the same edge list. Same layout as week 1, so this is really the week-1 web with an X-ray on. Code: analysis/week3b/.

Targeted attacks beat random failure — by a lot Step through the removals above and watch the web actually tear apart. Removing the highest-degree or highest-betweenness character first fragments the network roughly 40% faster than random removal. This is the textbook signature of a hub-dominated network: robust to random failure, fragile to targeted attack.
10.6% Drop in giant component from removing Spider-Man alone 303 → 271 characters. Real, but not catastrophic — the network has enough redundant connectivity underneath him to mostly survive.
No tie Degree and betweenness agree completely Spider-Man is #1 by both measures. Unlike the Adam Warlock surprise above (high out-degree, not high total degree), the single most-connected character is also the single most structurally load-bearing one.
Why one hub's removal isn't fatal 303 characters lose only 32 (10.6%) when Spider-Man is deleted — because most of his neighbors have other paths to the rest of the network through Hulk, Wolverine, Doctor Strange, and the dense core we mapped in week 1. A single hub matters, but this network isn't a star built around one node; it's a dense core with several redundant hubs.

The one place they diverge: Hercules and Black Widow Hercules (Marvel Comics) and Black Widow crack the betweenness top 10 without cracking the degree top 10 — modest connection counts, but they sit on unusually many shortest paths between other pairs of characters. They're quiet brokers, in the same structural role Adam Warlock plays for the network's farthest reaches.
How it's built nx.betweenness_centrality for the ranking; giant-component size recomputed after each single-node removal via nx.connected_components, on the undirected graph. Random baseline averaged over 30 shuffled removal orders. Code: analysis/week3d/.
